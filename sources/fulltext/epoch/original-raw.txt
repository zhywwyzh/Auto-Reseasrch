ABSTRACT
AI research agents are increasingly used to search over programs, mathematical
constructions, and proofs. However, existing systems typically optimize evaluator
feedback without adequately governing how that feedback is interpreted, chal-
lenged, and reused. As a result, promising but fragile candidates can be promoted
as discoveries, while benchmark improvements, finite certificates, and theorem-
level claims are too easily conflated. We introduce EPOCH, an evidence-governed
architecture designed to close this gap. EPOCH implements an evidence-governed
discovery loop by combining explicit task contracts, typed memory, active falsifica-
tion, admission checks, and independent replay, so that each candidate is evaluated
against the strength and scope of the claim it supports. EPOCH achieves state-of-
the-art aggregate performance on AlgoTune, substantially exceeding the strongest
baseline in mean normalized score (0.65 vs. 0.53), and attains the highest mean
score on the internal Math14 suite (0.57). It further shows favorable held-out be-
havior under official-test replay and leads the descriptive aggregate on AgentHPO.
Across ten discovery problems, EPOCH delivers substantial task-specific advances,
including improved executable constructions, optimized algorithms, counterexam-
ples, and proof-supported results. These advances demonstrate its ability to convert
search into concrete progress across mathematical and computational domains.
Together, the results suggest that evidence governance is a necessary step toward AI
research agents that produce not only stronger solutions, but also more trustworthy
scientific discoveries.
1 INTRODUCTION
AI research agents are increasingly being developed not only to solve well-specified tasks, but
also to generate candidate discoveries in code, algorithms, mathematics, and science. Recent
systems have demonstrated this potential across games, program synthesis, tensor decompositions,
geometric constructions, formal proofs, and scientific screening (Silver et al., 2018; Schrittwieser
et al., 2020; Li et al., 2022; Fawzi et al., 2022; Trinh et al., 2024; Hubert et al., 2025; Merchant et al.,
2023). FunSearch and AlphaEvolve further show that evaluator-guided evolution can transform large
language models into effective engines for open-ended search (Romera-Paredes et al., 2024; Novikov
et al., 2025). Despite this progress, current systems are primarily designed to generate and optimize
candidates, rather than to construct the evidence needed to support a discovery. A promising program,
construction, or conjecture is only a starting point: establishing its value may require identifying a
mechanism, producing a counterexample to a competing explanation, checking a finite certificate, or
distinguishing an experimentally supported observation from a proof-backed claim. These supporting
objects are often generated, tested, and recorded only opportunistically. As a result, an agent may
return an improved candidate without a clear account of why it works, what evidence supports it, or
how that evidence can be independently revisited. The central challenge is therefore to couple search
with an explicit process for constructing, challenging, and retaining the evidence that turns promising
candidates into supported discoveries.
Existing agents combine generation with execution, testing, or verification, but they do not provide
a common protocol for this evidence-construction process across domains. We ask: what protocol
should govern the promotion of a candidate into a retained discovery when the required supporting

We propose EPOCH (Evidence-conditioned Proposal Optimization through Cumulative Hypothesis-
governance, Fig. 1), an evidence-governed architecture that frames discovery as constrained search
over an explicit evidence ledger. Each task begins with a contract specifying permissible edits,
applicable checks, resource limits, and the scope of admissible claims. Typed memory retains both
successful and failed trials, allowing later search to reuse supporting evidence and avoid known
failure modes. Candidates are actively challenged against their declared mechanisms, admitted only
when their support satisfies the task contract, and independently replayed after the search endpoint is
frozen. Rather than making a verifier complete or elevating finite evidence into a general theorem,
EPOCH provides a domain-general protocol that aligns each retained result with its supporting
evidence and makes the limits of that support explicit.
Our contributions are:
• Evidence-governed discovery. We introduce a domain-general architecture in which task
contracts, typed memory, active falsification, claim-aware admission, and independent replay
jointly govern how candidate solutions become retained results.
• Strong exploration and exploitation performance. EPOCH achieves state-of-the-art
aggregate performance on AlgoTune, the highest mean score on internal Math14, favourable
official-test replay behaviour, and the leading descriptive aggregate on AgentHPO.
• Major discoveries with scoped evidence. Across ten discovery problems, EPOCH delivers
eleven improved tensor border-rank upper bounds, including an explicit 30-path construction
for Tss444; a reusable uniform Big-centroid construction; a record-setting 54-comparison
executable procedure for median-of-23 selection; a 1,512-gate (14.10%) matched-PyZX T-
count reduction for AES; a Lean-checked all-dimensional hypercube determinant inequality
with complete equality classification; and a Lean-checked nine-element counterexample to
the proposed Hβ coefficient ordering, extended to an infinite family.

2 RELATED WORK
Evaluator-Guided Search and Discovery. AlphaCode generates programs at scale and filters,
clusters, and reranks them according to their behavior on generated test inputs, whereas AlphaTensor
formulates matrix-multiplication algorithm discovery as a search for low-rank decompositions of the
matrix-multiplication tensor (Li et al., 2022; Fawzi et al., 2022; Google DeepMind, 2022). FunSearch
couples language-model proposals to evolutionary selection, and AlphaEvolve extends evaluator-
guided evolution to mathematical and computational tasks (Romera-Paredes et al., 2024; Novikov
et al., 2025). OpenEvolve, AdaEvolve, and AI-Scientist further broaden the landscape of evolutionary
and end-to-end research workflows (OpenEvolve contributors, 2026; Cemri et al., 2026; Lu et al.,
2024; Yamada et al., 2025). These systems establish the effectiveness of evaluator-guided search;
EPOCH complements them by introducing an evidence contract that governs which candidates may
be retained as discoveries, what support their claims require, and how rejected paths remain available
for subsequent search.
Domain-Specific Evidence for Discovery. AlphaGeometry combines learned construction with
symbolic deduction, AlphaProof searches formally checkable Lean states, and GNoME couples
learned screening to first-principles calculations (Trinh et al., 2024; Google DeepMind, 2024; Hubert
et al., 2025; Merchant et al., 2023; Google DeepMind, 2023). Their domain-specific validators are
essential rather than interchangeable. Recent AI-assisted mathematical studies likewise combine
model-guided exploration with expert scrutiny and, where applicable, formal verification, while
distinguishing suggestive evidence from established mathematical claims (Anthropic, 2026; OpenAI,
2026). EPOCH therefore does not seek to replace specialist validation with a universal truth score;
instead, it standardizes how domain-specific outputs support discovery. Typed receipts bind a retained
result to the checks that produced its evidence, three-valued gates distinguish supported, refuted, and
unresolved candidates, and independent replay tests frozen artifacts outside the search loop. The
result is a replayable admission record that specifies what was checked, what outcome was obtained,
and which claim the available evidence permits.

4 EVALUATION
We first compare endpoint quality and held-out behavior, then examine component removals and the
distribution of performance across tasks. The discovery audit in Section 5 asks a separate question:
what reusable objects and bounded claims emerge from this process?
4.1 BENCHMARKS AND ESTIMANDS
We evaluate EPOCH in three complementary settings: AlgoTune for constrained algorithmic search,
Math14 for diverse mathematical objectives with exact endpoint checkers, and six AgentHPOBench
tasks for component ablations (AlgoTune contributors, 2026; OpenMOSS, 2026). On a frozen 12-task
AlgoTune subset, five methods receive the same budget of 48 semantic calls and are run with three
seeds; we report mean task scores and their macro-average. To test whether search-time gains transfer
beyond development data, we freeze each eligible final program and replay it, without further search
or adaptation, on 32 previously unused official test inputs. This replay reruns correctness checks
and fresh-process timing. Math14 evaluates performance across 14 mathematical tasks, whereas
AgentHPO isolates the contribution of EPOCH’s individual components. Full task-level results, seeds,
measurements, and statistical procedures are provided in the Supplement.
4.2 SEARCH PERFORMANCE AND HELD-OUT ROBUSTNESS
EPOCH achieves the highest AlgoTune development mean (Table 1), reaching 0.65 and surpassing
the strongest baseline, AdaEvolve, by 22.60%. It also exceeds both AI-Scientist variants and
OpenEvolve by margins ranging from 24.80% to 30.90%, establishing the strongest aggregate
performance among the evaluated methods. This advantage extends to the held-out official-test replay:
after endpoints are frozen and evaluated without further search or adaptation, EPOCH has positive
mean effects against all four baselines. Maximum common subgraph retains a +0.48 normalized-
score advantage and Convolve2D a +0.24 advantage, although NMF reverses to −0.27. The largest
development gains occur on Convolve2D, maximum common subgraph, Robertson ODE, NMF, and
zeta-zero count, reflecting task-specific improvements to the evaluated executable endpoints under
the benchmark protocol.
On Math14 (Table 2), EPOCH achieves the highest mean score of 0.57, outperforming every
evaluated baseline. Its relative improvement ranges from 0.60% over OpenEvolve to 10.90% over AI-
Scientist v1, demonstrating that EPOCH’s evidence-governed search architecture transfers effectively
from executable optimization to diverse mathematical objectives.

5.1 CONSTRUCTIVE DISCOVERIES AND ALGORITHMIC OPTIMIZATION
Characteristic-two tensor transfer. Low-border-rank decompositions are fundamental building
blocks for fast bilinear algorithms: each explicit construction provides a concrete certificate of
reduced algebraic complexity. A central difficulty is that rational decompositions can collapse after
reduction to characteristic two, preventing their direct use over the target field. EPOCH turns this
obstruction into a constructive search signal: it extracts the incompatible coefficient constraints,
uses their witnesses to select a new degeneration chart, and searches for a valid characteristic-two
replacement. Across the audit, this procedure transfers all 43 directly applicable formulas and covers
24 coordinate-distinct targets, yielding eleven improved border bounds. In particular, it discovers
a 30-path construction for Tss444, establishing R(Tss444) ≤ 30. The result contributes explicit low-
complexity decompositions in a regime where naïve transfer fails, expanding the constructive toolkit
available for algebraic-complexity and fast-algorithm design.
Uniform Big-centroid construction. Big-centroid constructions are particularly valuable when
they extend across a family of dimensions: unlike an isolated decomposition, a uniform family can
serve as a reusable object for asymptotic border-rank and algebraic-complexity analysis. EPOCH
discovers such a family, deriving an explicit q + u1-term construction, where q =

reusable left-inverse identity rather than repeated ambient linear solves. The resulting formula applies
throughout the stated parameter and field regimes, replacing a sequence of instance-specific searches
with a single dimension-general construction. This shift from individual examples to an explicit
scalable family provides a concrete foundation for studying the associated complexity bounds and for
developing subsequent centroid-based tensor constructions.

6 DISCUSSION AND LIMITATIONS
The benchmarks and portfolio support complementary conclusions. Aggregate gains show that
contracts, typed memory, falsification, admission, and replay can guide search, while the cases show
how failures become repair strategies and verification turns candidates into scoped research artifacts.
This evidence discipline is particularly relevant to AI4Math and AI4Science workflows combining
code, symbolic reasoning, simulation, and formal methods.
Two limitations define the next frontier. First, larger paired evaluations with fixed budgets and
comprehensive resource accounting are needed. Second, although EPOCH makes evidential gaps
explicit, it does not resolve them all; specialist claims still require external review, statement alignment,
and priority assessment. Extending the protocol to simulations, experiments, databases, scientific
instruments, and expert interaction remains an important direction.
7 CONCLUSION
EPOCH reframes AI research agents from candidate generators into systems for evidence-guided
discovery. By combining typed memory, active falsification, admission, and independent replay,
EPOCH turns successful, failed, and repaired search trajectories into retained results with explicit
supporting evidence. Across ten discovery problems, this process yields concrete advances: eleven im-
proved tensor border bounds, a 1,512-T-gate reduction in an AES reversible circuit, a 54-comparison
median-of-23 procedure that improves the classical 55-comparison Hadian–Sobel bound (Hadian &
Sobel, 1969), a sharp all-dimensional hypercube inequality with an equality characterization, and
an infinite family refuting the proposed Hβ front-loading property. Together with state-of-the-art
aggregate results on AlgoTune and leading performance on Math14 and AgentHPO, these outcomes
show that evidence-governed search can do more than optimize benchmark scores: it can generate
substantive algorithmic and mathematical discoveries.

D DETAILED ARCHITECTURE AND EVIDENCE PROTOCOL
This section translates the main-text contract into protocol-level operations. The mathematical
notation defines the quantities to preserve and verify, while the implementation details specify the
concrete settings through which the architecture enforces its invariants.
D.1 THE RESEARCH TASK AS AN EXECUTABLE CONTRACT
A task is more than an objective function. It specifies an initial artifact, editable scope, allowed
observations, correctness conditions, resource limits, and an endpoint rule. For a code task, the
artifact may be a Python implementation of a function with a fixed input/output interface. For a
tensor task, it may be a list of rank-one paths and a target-coordinate generator. For a mathematical
statement, it includes the hypotheses and the meaning of any finite or formal evidence used to justify
the conclusion. Without this surrounding contract, a high score is compatible with changing the
problem rather than solving it.
The public view and terminal view have different roles. Public instances can influence proposal
generation. A terminal view can evaluate the selected endpoint only under the declared protocol. If
official test feedback is used to generate a new candidate, that candidate belongs to a new search
experiment; it is not the original endpoint’s replay. Similarly, a certificate verifier may expose a
precise failed coordinate during search, whereas a held-out benchmark evaluation must not silently
become an additional optimization loop.
A resource envelope has both accounting and attribution semantics. Candidate execution consumes
task resources; proposal generation consumes semantic calls and associated model resources. A
candidate that exceeds a valid registered time limit has failed within the method contract. An evaluator
that cannot launch because a required device is unavailable creates a different state. The experiment
must identify which boundary failed before deciding whether the cell has a score, or a fallback.
This distinction is particularly consequential when comparing methods with different orchestration
patterns.
D.2 EVIDENCE RECORDS AND INFORMATION EXPOSED TO GENERATION
An event should be interpretable independently of the model’s narrative. It identifies the exact
executable or symbolic candidate, evaluator version, and input version; metric and correctness fields
record different outcomes; and attribution explains why the result can or cannot be used. An evidence
reference points to this immutable event, not to a mutable natural-language statement such as “the
previous attempt worked.” A subsequent claim can therefore be traced back to a specific observation
without printing storage-level identifiers in the paper.
The bounded memory view is a projection of a longer evidence history. A projection can contain
successful trials, rejected proposals, baseline observations, and relevant counterexamples without
reproducing an entire transcript. This distinction matters because a context-window summary may
omit details while the underlying record still retains them. The architecture’s auditability claim
concerns preservation of the full record and stable identifiers, not an assertion that the model always
receives every historical event.
16
EPOCH: arXiv preprint
A useful proposal has at least four interpretable parts. Its hypothesis describes the mechanism intended
to improve the objective. Its expected effect states the direction or observable change. Its evidence
references identify earlier observations supporting the intervention. Its falsifier specifies a condition
under which the mechanism should be rejected. For example, a faster convolution implementation
may predict identical zero-padded linear convolution on rectangular shapes; a wraparound mismatch
is a falsifier even when timing improves. An algebraic lift may predict a specified coefficient identity;
a single mismatching semantic coordinate refutes that proposal.
Evidence can be useful without being positive. A tensor-chart obstruction excludes an entire local
representation. A failure at a boundary input shows which invariant an optimization omitted. A
repaired parser incident explains why a method appeared to produce no candidates. These observations
should be stored with different types because they imply different next actions. Treating them all as
low rewards loses both scientific information and the ability to diagnose the evaluation itself.

Scope checks that changes remain within the task. Budget checks enforce the candidate’s resource
contract. Correctness checks the task semantics. Challenges test declared weak points or adverse
cases. Replay checks reproducibility of the selected object.

endpoint can be admitted as correct on the registered validation instances without being admitted as
universally correct on all inputs. A polynomial identity checked symbolically can support all values

D.4 PROTOCOL-LEVEL DISCOVERY LOOP
The following pseudocode exposes the sequence of decisions without adding an unreported search
heuristic.
Input: frozen contract, initial verified fallback when supplied, resource ledger, and empty
append-only trial history.
Initialize: record the baseline, its verification receipt, and its resource use.
While an intervention remains allowed:
Choose the current phase using the registered controller.
Construct the bounded evidence view and request a proposal.
Validate its schema, editable scope, and required evidence fields.
Evaluate correctness, objective, and the applicable challenge.
Attribute failures before deciding score eligibility.
Append the candidate, observations, and gate reasons to history.
Replace the endpoint only if admission and the comparison rule permit.
Finalize: reconstruct and replay the exact selected artifact.
Seal: bind the final claim, scope, contract, history, and replay receipts.
The pseudocode deliberately separates evaluator observations from endpoint replacement. A candidate
can be informative and still be ineligible for replacement.
D.5 ENDPOINT SAFETY AND PROVENANCE INVARIANTS
Suppose the baseline is verified under contract C and every endpoint replacement requires all
applicable gates to pass. By induction on the number of interventions, the retained endpoint always
has a passing receipt under that contract. At the base step this is the registered baseline. At a later
step, a failed or unresolved candidate does not replace it, while an admitted replacement has its own
passing receipt. This invariant explains why a misleading public score need not corrupt the endpoint.
The invariant is intentionally relative. If the correctness checker misses a class of inputs, a passing
endpoint may still fail outside its registered coverage. If the checker itself is wrong, the invariant
establishes protocol compliance rather than external truth. This is why the artifact must preserve
the evaluator identity and why independent semantic reconstruction provides information beyond
repeated execution of the same checker.
Append-only history gives a second invariant. A new event extends the history from Mt to (Mt, et+1);
it does not change the candidate or attribution of earlier events.
Claim sealing adds a binding invariant rather than a theorem of correctness. If the candidate, evaluator,
inputs, or claim text changes, the archival record must create a distinct version. This versioning rule
does not validate a theorem; it only ensures that a receipt cannot silently migrate to a different claim
or artifact.
D.6 FALSIFICATION, DUPLICATES, AND PHASE CHANGES
A repeated configuration can indicate useful replication, accidental repetition, or a failure to incorpo-
rate evidence. The AgentHPO trace metric counts equality with an earlier emitted configuration; it
does not by itself distinguish those motivations. Consequently, a lower repetition rate is an operational
signal, not a standalone measure of scientific efficiency. In the deterministic traps, the intended
repeated behavior is specified by construction; in external tasks, endpoint outcomes remain necessary
to interpret it.
A falsifier differs from an ordinary correctness test by being directed at the proposal’s mechanism. A
factor-sharing proposal must preserve ANF coverage and return ancillas; a certificate compression

proposal must preserve all represented cases; a conjectured inequality must survive degenerate and
equality regimes. The same challenge can discover a scientific failure and produce a useful input for
the next proposal.
Phase changes organize how these observations affect search. Exploration seeks materially different
hypotheses. Refinement invests in supported mechanisms. Finalization stops search and concentrates
on replay and claim scope.
D.7 INDEPENDENT REPLAY AND TRUSTED BOUNDARIES
Replay independence has several levels. Process independence reconstructs the same candidate
in a clean process. Implementation independence reconstructs the mathematical or executable
target using another program. Representation independence uses a different computational model,
such as multiplication-matrix norms versus resultants, or one irreducible finite-field model versus
another. Formal reflection moves selected finite checks into a proof assistant. These levels can be
complementary rather than interchangeable.
A strong replay package makes the interfaces explicit: exact input object, output statement, invariant
checked, failure code, and resource boundary. For example, a QASM truth-table replay checks an
emitted circuit; it does not verify the optimizer’s claim of global minimality. A Lean determinant
kernel proves an implication from a characteristic-polynomial hypothesis; it does not supply the
representation-theoretic hypothesis. An exhaustive graph-catalogue pass verifies every listed record;
it does not prove the catalogue contains every possible graph.
This layered view is also how the supplementary catalogue should be read. Each entry identifies the
mathematical object and the strongest retained statement, then distinguishes construction, exhaustive
finite checks, analytic arguments, and formal kernels. Results that are locally complete but globally
unresolved remain useful evidence when their boundary is preserved.

I.2.1 WHY ARBITRARY-ARITY SHARING DIFFERS FROM PAIR MATCHING
A pairwise optimizer considers sharing between two residual expressions at a time. A factor that
benefits three or more groups can be unprofitable in every locally selected pair and still profitable
when its opening cost is paid only once for the whole group. The binary variable yf represents this
global activation decision, while the xgf variables assign residual use. Their coupling captures a cost
that cannot be represented by independent residual choices alone.

The optimization model also distinguishes logical sharing from reversible execution. A classical

includes the registered compute/uncompute expense. A model that omits the inverse computation
would optimize the wrong cost contract.
I.2.2 CONSTRUCTING A CIRCUIT FROM A SELECTED ASSIGNMENT
For each opened factor, construct its value on a designated ancilla using the registered factor imple-
mentation. Apply the residual-specific controlled operations to the assigned outputs in the scheduled
order. Because the factor register is used as a control and its input data remain available under
the contract, run the inverse factor computation to reset that ancilla. The emitted schedule is then
translated to QASM and checked as a complete circuit.
This procedure requires more than an optimal integer-program objective. The assignment must
correspond to the intended ANF occurrences; every occurrence must be accounted for under the
representation’s cancellation convention; control dependencies must be legal; and uncomputation must
preserve outputs. A solver can be correct while the schedule generator is wrong. The independent
ANF and QASM layers address these different interfaces.
The Boolean identity follows by distributivity in the two-element Boolean ring. For bits, XOR is
addition and AND is multiplication, so (a + b)h = ah + bh. Applying the identity at each output
index justifies shared algebraic factors. The formal identity does not encode the entire ancilla schedule
or a decomposition of every CCX into T gates, which is why the full-circuit and cost checks remain
separate.

A formula containing division is meaningful only when its denominators are nonzero. The constructor

As an algorithmic result, Big-centroid is a deterministic generator producing factor paths and a

I.4.1 DECISION-TREE CORRECTNESS VERSUS PATH-COUNT ARITHMETIC
A path count is not a decision-tree correctness proof. Every comparison branch must represent the
corresponding order relation, every terminal return must have the requested rank in every total order
extending that relation, and every dispatch must preserve the target rank. The global count becomes
meaningful only after these semantic conditions are attached to the enumerated states.
Write a residual state as (P, r, b), where P is the current comparison poset, r the requested zero-based
rank, and b the remaining comparison budget. Define C(P, r, b) recursively: a leaf is valid when its
returned element has rank r in every linear extension of P; an internal comparison of incomparable
x, y is valid when both child states extend P by the corresponding relation and satisfy C with budget
at most b − 1. Induction on b then gives
C(P, r, b) =⇒ every total order extending P reaches a correct leaf in at most b comparisons.
(S16)
The base case is the universal-rank condition at a leaf. For the inductive step, every total order satisfies
exactly one of x < y or y < x, so execution enters the matching child; its budget is smaller, and the
induction hypothesis supplies correctness and termination. Thus the semantic theorem depends on
child-state validity and leaf ranks, not on the number of enumerated paths.
A residual problem can be represented by a finite poset and a target rank. Removing an element
certified below the target decrements the target rank; removing an element certified above it leaves
the rank unchanged. Canonical relabeling is valid only when it is a bijection preserving order
constraints and transporting the selected item and target correctly. These simple rules explain why
rank-decrement and relabel corruptions are important negative controls: either mistake can preserve
graph sizes and node counts while changing the selection problem.
For a policy node with remaining budget b, each comparison child must have a smaller budget, and
its state must extend the parent’s comparison outcome. A solved leaf must satisfy the registered rank
criterion. Induction on the budget then verifies termination and correctness of the residual policy. The
supplied Lean stack formalizes these semantic ingredients and a large generated policy library; it
does not allow the remaining concrete census-binding obligations to be omitted.
